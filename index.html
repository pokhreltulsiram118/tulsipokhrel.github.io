About

Where the questions started.

I grew up in Dang, Nepal, in a place with no shortage of water — rivers, streams, groundwater everywhere you looked. But growing up, I also saw the other side of that abundance: communities without safe drinking water, no testing of water quality at the source, and the recurring damage of floods in vulnerable regions.

It wasn't a lack of resources. It was a lack of management. That contradiction became clear to me during my undergraduate studies in Agricultural Engineering, and it's what pulled me toward hydrology — the idea that solving water problems in a resource-rich but management-poor context takes more than awareness. It takes rigorous, applied research: modeling and data that show where interventions actually pay off, efficiently and economically.

That's the throughline connecting my work now — from watershed modeling in South Dakota to drought assessment back home in Nepal.
